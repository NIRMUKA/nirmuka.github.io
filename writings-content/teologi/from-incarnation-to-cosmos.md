From Incarnation to Cosmos

The Total Trans-Existence of God-Human in Christ and the Network of
God-Human-Creation Relation

Abstrak

This study investigates the profound relationship between God, humanity,
and the universe, a central theme in theological discourse.
Traditionally, the concept of the Incarnation, where God becomes human
in the person of Jesus Christ, has been understood within historical and
theological contexts. This research proposes a broader perspective
through the concept of trans-existence, which views the Incarnation as a
cosmic phenomenon affecting all existence. By exploring the dynamic and
interconnected network of relationships between God, humanity, and
creation, this study responds to modern scientific advancements and
ecological awareness, emphasizing the urgency of reevaluating
traditional theology in light of these developments. The research argues
that the Incarnation, understood through trans-existence, challenges the
static view of existence and highlights the interconnectedness and
interdependence of all creation. This perspective aligns with modern
cosmology, which depicts the universe as an interconnected entity, and
underscores the theological view of humanity's role as stewards of the
earth. The study aims to produce a holistic and integrative
understanding of existence, reflecting the interconnected reality of
life, thereby enriching theological insights and contributing to
environmental conservation and social harmony. By integrating insights
from theology, philosophy, science, and ecology, this research offers a
comprehensive view of the Incarnation's implications for the entire
cosmos. It promotes an ethical and practical response to the global
environmental crisis, encouraging a shift from exploitative to
cooperative and from destructive to constructive interactions with the
natural world.

Keyword: Incarnation, Trans-Existence, God-Human Relation, Cosmos,
Theology

Introduction

The background of this study is rooted in the fundamental question of
the relationship between God, humanity, and the universe, a central
theme in theology throughout history. In various religious traditions,
particularly within Christianity, the concept of the Incarnation---where
God becomes human in the person of Jesus Christ---represents one of the
deepest mysteries, illustrating God's closeness and involvement with His
creation. However, the meaning and implications of the Incarnation are
often understood in a limited way, primarily within historical and
theological contexts. Many theologians and spiritual thinkers view the
Incarnation as the culmination of God's relationship with humanity, but
they have yet to fully explore its impact on the entire cosmos and every
aspect of existence.

The approach of trans-existence offers a broader perspective, viewing
the Incarnation not only as a historical event but as a cosmic
phenomenon that impacts all of existence. In this view, the relationship
between God, humanity, and creation is not static but a dynamic network
of interconnected and interdependent relationships. This idea challenges
us to see that every action and existence in the universe is part of a
larger web of relationships, connecting God with all His creation.

This research also emerges in response to the advancements in modern
science and the deepening ecological awareness that demand a revision
and expansion of traditional theology. Science reveals the complexity
and wonder of the universe, while the global environmental crisis
underscores the importance of understanding our relationship with
nature. In this context, exploring the network of God-human-creation
relations becomes increasingly relevant and urgent. For instance, modern
cosmological theories depict the universe as an interconnected entity,
where each part influences and is influenced by the whole. This
perspective aligns with the theological view of trans-existence,
emphasizing the interconnectedness and interdependence of all creation.

Moreover, the growing ecological awareness demands that we reexamine
humanity's role as stewards of the earth. The global environmental
crisis, including climate change, biodiversity loss, and pollution, are
clear signs that our relationship with nature is in turmoil. In light of
trans-existential theology, this crisis can be understood as a
disruption in the network of relationships between God, humanity, and
creation. Therefore, restoring this relationship becomes crucial not
only theologically but also practically for the sustainability of life
on earth.

Through this study, it is hoped to produce a more holistic and
integrative understanding of existence, reflecting the reality of the
interconnectedness of all aspects of life. Thus, we not only enrich
theological insights but also contribute perspectives that can
strengthen efforts for environmental conservation and social harmony, in
line with the spiritual calling to protect and care for God's creation.
This research aims to inspire concrete actions based on a deep awareness
of our interconnectedness with the entire universe, encouraging a
paradigm shift from exploitative to cooperative, from destructive to
constructive.

In an era where the boundaries between science and religion are
increasingly blurred, and the need for interdisciplinary collaboration
becomes more urgent, an integrative theological approach such as
proposed in this study can be a crucial bridge. By combining insights
from theology, philosophy, science, and ecology, we can develop a more
comprehensive and transformative view of our role in the cosmos and how
we can live in harmony with the divine principles that govern all
creation.

What is Trans-Existence?

Trans-Existence is a philosophical concept that poses fundamental
questions about the nature of existence and the possibility for
individuals or entities to surpass the limitations of conventional
existence. In this concept, "trans" indicates change or transition,
while "Existence" refers to the process of becoming existent or evolving
into a particular form of existence. First and foremost, to understand
Trans-Existence, we need to explore the notion of existence itself.

Existence, as a concept, has been a central theme in philosophical
discourse for millennia. It encompasses not only the state of being but
also the processes and conditions under which something comes to be.
Traditional views of existence often focus on physical presence and
material reality, as defined by philosophers such as Aristotle and
Descartes. Aristotle's notion of "being qua being" investigates the
essence of entities, while Descartes' famous dictum "Cogito, ergo sum"
("I think, therefore I am") emphasizes self-awareness as the foundation
of existence.

However, Trans-Existence challenges these traditional views by
suggesting that existence is not static or confined to materiality.
Instead, it is a dynamic and fluid process, marked by continuous
transition and transformation. This idea aligns with existentialist
thought, particularly the work of philosophers like Jean-Paul Sartre and
Martin Heidegger, who emphasize the fluid and contingent nature of human
existence. Sartre's notion of "being-for-itself" and Heidegger's concept
of "Being-in-the-world" both highlight the idea that existence is an
ongoing project, shaped by our choices, actions, and interactions with
the world. But, let us speak first the term existence itself.

Existence

I'm gonna start with Dostoevesky, his conception of existence is
characterized by a deep engagement with the inner lives of his
characters, portraying their intense moral and existential dilemmas. He
explores the tumultuous inner landscapes of human consciousness,
revealing the complexities of individual identity, free will, and the
profound struggle for spiritual and moral meaning. Through his
narratives, Dostoevsky delves into the darkest recesses of human
psychology, probing the depths of guilt, suffering, and redemption,
while also examining the existential tension between faith and doubt,
reason and passion. His works, Especially Notes From the Underground
underscore the existential condition as a profound journey of
self-discovery and moral reckoning, where individuals confront the
complexities of their own existence with unflinching introspection and
moral urgency.

Next is Kierkegaard. According to Kierkegaard, existence is a highly
subjective and individualistic state that cannot be reduced to universal
truths or abstract knowledge. He emphasizes the importance of individual
subjective experience over philosophical systems or objective truths.
Kierkegaard views human freedom to make choices and take responsibility
for actions as crucial. He argues that existence involves anxiety and
uncertainty, where individuals must make significant choices freely,
without relying on external authorities or predetermined paths. In terms
of religion, Kierkegaard considers a personal, passionate relationship
with God that transcends rational proofs or collective beliefs,
emphasizing individual faith and commitment. He introduces the concept
of the "teleological suspension of the ethical," suggesting that ethical
norms and social conventions may need to be set aside in rare instances
to follow a higher ethical or religious calling.

According to Martin Heidegger, existence is fundamentally about the
experience of being-in-the-world (Dasein). Heidegger shifts
philosophical focus from traditional metaphysical questions to the
nature of human existence and its relationship with Being itself. He
critiques the Cartesian separation of subject and object, arguing
instead for a holistic understanding where human beings are always
already engaged with the world around them.

For Heidegger, Dasein's existence is characterized by temporality, where
the past, present, and future are interwoven in each individual's
understanding of being. He emphasizes the importance of authenticity,
urging individuals to confront their own mortality and the possibilities
inherent in their existence. This confrontation with mortality, or
"being-towards-death," reveals the finite nature of human existence and
underscores the urgency of living authentically.

Jean-Paul Sartre's concept of existence revolves around the idea of
radical freedom and responsibility. For Sartre, existence precedes
essence, meaning that human beings are first thrown into the world
without a predetermined purpose or essence. Unlike objects whose essence
precedes their existence (e.g., a chair designed for a specific
function), human beings are characterized by their conscious ability to
choose and define themselves through their actions.

According to Sartre, individuals are condemned to be free, which implies
that they are always faced with the burden of making choices. This
freedom is not just the ability to choose between alternatives but
extends to the fundamental ability to shape one's own existence. In this
existentialist framework, there is no inherent meaning or purpose
imposed on individuals from external sources such as God or nature.
Instead, each person must actively create meaning through their choices
and actions.

Paul Tillich's understanding of existence delves deeply into the idea of
"standing out" or "to exist" from non-being. He interprets existence as
a process where entities move from potential being, which he refers to
as relative non-being, towards actual being. This transition involves
the realization of inherent possibilities and potentialities. However,
Tillich emphasizes that existence is marked by a fundamental
incompleteness or finitude. Even in achieving actuality, entities retain
a connection to non-being---they do not exhaust all their potentialities
nor fully escape the limitations of existence.

For Tillich, existence encompasses a dynamic interplay between being and
non-being. It signifies not only the act of coming into being but also
the ongoing struggle against non-being. This struggle manifests as a
tension between actuality and potentiality, where entities continuously
strive to manifest their latent capacities while facing the resistance
posed by their inherent limitations. Thus, existence, in Tillich's view,
is characterized by this dual movement of standing out from non-being
into being while simultaneously remaining interconnected with the realm
of non-being.

Existence, in the philosophical discourse shaped by Dostoevsky,
Kierkegaard, Heidegger, Sartre, and Tillich, offers profound insights
into the human condition, yet each perspective also reveals its
limitations. Dostoevsky, through works like "Notes From the
Underground," portrays existence as a profound moral and existential
journey, delving into intricate inner conflicts and moral dilemmas.
However, his deep focus on individual psychology sometimes obscures a
broader existential view, portraying extreme experiences that may not
universally represent the diversity of human existential experiences.

Kierkegaard emphasizes the subjective nature of existence and the
critical role of individual freedom in making life choices. While his
focus on subjective experience contributes significantly to existential
philosophy, it often sidelines universal aspects relevant to all
individuals. Moreover, his concept of the "teleological suspension of
the ethical" raises serious ethical questions about actions that diverge
from socially accepted norms or ethics. Heidegger's concept of "Dasein"
and human existence within the world highlights the importance of
authenticity. Yet, his complex theoretical framework and dense language
pose challenges for many in comprehending his concepts fully.
Furthermore, his focus on existential ontology sometimes struggles to
connect with practical applications in everyday life.

Jean-Paul Sartre, with his theory of radical freedom, asserts that
existence precedes essence, giving humans absolute freedom to define
themselves through their choices and actions. However, the logical
consequence of this view is the absence of inherent meaning or purpose
imposed externally, which can pose significant challenges in creating a
stable or consistent moral or existential framework amidst life's
complexities. Paul Tillich introduces the idea of existence as a
struggle between being and non-being, emphasizing a dynamic process from
potentiality to actuality. Yet, the philosophical language and concepts
he employs often prove challenging to articulate and apply practically.
While Tillich offers a profound understanding of existence's existential
struggle, its complexity can hinder broader or more universal
comprehension of human existence.

Existence is not merely a process of transitioning from non-being to
being, or from potentiality to actuality. Instead, existence is an
absolute state that knows no boundaries or distinctions between
existence and non-existence. According to this view, existence is not a
phenomenon that needs to be contrasted with non-existence, because there
is no possible contrast between "being" and "not being."

In this understanding, existence is eternal and unquestionable.
Existence does not require justification or a search for meaning,
because existence itself is an indisputable reality. There is no journey
towards a more real or meaningful existence, because existence already
encompasses everything and requires no further explanation. This
perspective rejects the idea that existence involves a struggle or
battle with non-being or unrealized possibilities. Instead, existence is
seen as a complete and perfect state in itself, without the need to
challenge or overcome something outside of itself.

Trans-Existence

Trans-existence, within the framework of this understanding of
existence, refers to a state or condition that transcends traditional
notions of existence and non-existence altogether. It denotes a mode of
being that surpasses the binary opposition between being and non-being,
acknowledging existence as an inherent and unquestionable reality that
encompasses all possibilities without the need for contrast or struggle.

In this context, trans-existence implies a state that transcends the
duality of potentiality and actuality. It envisions a mode of being
where existence is not seen as contingent upon circumstances or defined
by limitations but rather as an eternal and complete reality. This
conception of existence surpasses the ordinary distinctions and
constraints that typically characterize our understanding of being. It
suggests a realm where the conventional boundaries and
oppositions---such as those between potentiality and actuality, or
existence and non-existence---are dissolved.

In this transcendent state, existence is recognized as an inherent and
absolute reality, encompassing all possibilities within itself without
necessitating contrast, conflict, or any form of justification. This
boundless and unqualified form of being is not dependent on external
validation or explanation; it is self-sufficient and self-evident. Such
a state surpasses the ordinary, finite experiences of existence,
entering into a domain of ultimate completeness and wholeness. Here, the
usual limitations of time, space, and identity are transcended, leading
to an experience of existence that is infinite and unconditioned, free
from the dualistic frameworks that typically define human understanding.

Trans-Existence and its relation to Incarnation

Trans-existence, as understood here, posits existence beyond the
dichotomy of being and non-being, implying a state that transcends
conventional limits and encompasses all possibilities. Incarnation, in
theological terms, particularly within Christian doctrine, signifies the
embodiment of the divine in human form, specifically referring to Jesus
Christ as the incarnate Son of God.

The concept of Incarnation can be viewed as a total trans-existence
because it signifies the union of the divine and human natures in Jesus
Christ. It represents a complete transcendence of ordinary human
existence by the divine, where the divine essence enters into and fully
inhabits human existence without losing its divine attributes. This
union is not merely a transition from non-being to being or from
potentiality to actuality, but a profound transformation that goes
beyond ordinary human existence.

Critically, this understanding challenges the conventional understanding
of existence as a struggle or journey towards actualization. Instead,
Incarnation presents a radical unity where the divine and human coexist
in a single person, transcending the limitations of both human and
divine existence alone. It embodies the idea that existence itself can
be understood in terms of a seamless integration and harmony, where the
divine transcends into the human without diminishing its divine nature.

Thus, Incarnation as a total trans-existence redefines existence by
emphasizing unity over duality, transcendence over struggle, and
completeness over potentiality. It invites reflection on the nature of
existence in relation to the divine, suggesting that true existence may
involve a state of being that surpasses ordinary human comprehension and
categories of existence.

The Concept of Incarnation

According to Britannica, The term "Incarnation" (from the Latin "caro,"
meaning "flesh") refers to the union of the divine nature of the second
person of the Trinity with human nature, either at the moment this union
became operative in the Virgin Mary's womb or as a permanent reality in
the person of Jesus. This concept is closely linked to the claim in the
Gospel of John that the Word became flesh, meaning the preexistent Word
took on human nature. The core of the doctrine is that the preexistent
Word is embodied in Jesus of Nazareth, who is depicted in the Gospel of
John as being in intimate union with the Father, conveying the Father's
words in his teachings.

How Christ Incarnation become the Total Trans-Existence of God-Human

Christ's incarnation as the model of trans-existence represents a
profound and transformative union of divine and human natures in the
person of Jesus Christ. This concept, deeply rooted in Christian
theology, suggests that through the incarnation, God fully experiences
human existence---embracing its joys, sufferings, and
limitations---while simultaneously maintaining divine attributes. This
unique fusion allows for a comprehensive understanding and
transformation of human nature from within.

In the incarnation, God, who is infinite and eternal, enters into the
finite and temporal realm of human existence. This is not a mere
temporary or partial indwelling but a complete and permanent union known
as the hypostatic union, where the divine and human natures coexist
without confusion, change, division, or separation. Jesus Christ
embodies this union perfectly, being fully God and fully human. As fully
human, Jesus experiences all aspects of human life. He feels the full
range of human emotions, including joy, love, sorrow, and pain. He
experiences physical needs and limitations, such as hunger, thirst,
fatigue, and the need for rest. Jesus also endures profound suffering,
most notably in his passion and crucifixion, which includes physical
pain, emotional anguish, and spiritual desolation.

Despite these human experiences, Jesus does not relinquish his divine
nature. Throughout his life, he demonstrates divine attributes,
performing miracles, forgiving sins, and possessing omniscience and
omnipotence. This simultaneous existence in both natures---fully God and
fully human---creates a unique state of being that transcends
conventional human limitations while fully engaging with them. This
state of trans-existence exemplifies how the divine and human can be
fully integrated, allowing for a transformative experience that
encompasses all facets of human existence.

The implications of this union are profound. Through the incarnation,
God not only participates in human life but also redeems and transforms
it from within. By uniting the divine with the human, Jesus provides a
bridge between humanity and God. This bridge is not merely a symbolic
connection but a real and tangible transformation of human nature.
Jesus' life, death, and resurrection are acts that infuse human
existence with divine purpose and possibility. His life serves as the
model of perfect humanity, demonstrating how to live in complete
alignment with God's will. His teachings, actions, and attitudes provide
a template for ethical and moral living, emphasizing love, compassion,
justice, and humility.

Jesus' crucifixion represents the ultimate act of divine love and
self-sacrifice. By willingly suffering and dying on the cross, Jesus
atones for human sin, offering redemption and reconciliation with God.
This act of atonement is not just a legal transaction but a
transformative event that changes the very nature of human existence,
opening the way for eternal life with God. The resurrection of Jesus is
the definitive act of trans-existence. It demonstrates victory over
death and the promise of eternal life. The resurrection is a foretaste
of the resurrection that believers hope to share, signifying the
transformation of mortal bodies into glorified, eternal ones. This event
transcends mere human experience, showcasing the ultimate triumph of
life over death and the fulfillment of divine purpose.

Moreover, Christ's incarnation has profound implications for the entire
cosmos. It signifies that the divine is not distant but deeply involved
in the material world. This involvement suggests a renewed understanding
of human identity and purpose. Humanity is invited into a relationship
with the divine that transforms ordinary existence into a participation
in divine life. This perspective calls for a holistic and integrative
approach to theology that considers the interconnectedness of all
creation. It emphasizes the sanctity of the entire cosmos, recognizing
that God's presence in the material world elevates and redeems creation.

Understanding Christ's incarnation as trans-existence encourages ethical
and practical responses. It challenges humanity to live in a way that
reflects the divine involvement in the world, promoting justice,
compassion, and care for creation. This perspective fosters a sense of
responsibility towards the environment and a commitment to sustainable
living, recognizing the sacredness of all life. It calls for a renewed
commitment to social justice, emphasizing the need to address issues of
poverty, inequality, and oppression. Believers are encouraged to see
their lives as part of a larger divine narrative, where every action and
decision contributes to the realization of God's kingdom on earth.

Christ's incarnation as the model of trans-existence represents the
profound union of divine and human natures, transcending conventional
boundaries and transforming human and cosmic existence. Through this
union, God experiences human life in all its facets, transforms human
nature from within, and infuses human existence with divine purpose and
possibility. This theological concept challenges believers to see their
lives as part of a divine narrative, encouraging them to live ethically
and compassionately, reflecting the transformative power of the
incarnation in their daily lives.
